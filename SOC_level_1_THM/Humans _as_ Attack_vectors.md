# Humans as Attack Vectors

Humans-----> The **weakest link in cyber security** and those who help threat actors the most.

Instead of taking time for bypassing firewall defenses, attackers easy way to get access is phishing one of the employee on the network.

## Why Humans Are Targeted

Humans are targeted because of the **access** they can provide - to websites, mailboxes, or databases.

Examples:-
1. Breach Google account of the HR manager ------> Steal and sell the entire employee database.
2. Trick a wealthy person into running malware-------> Hijack a web banking session from their PC.
3. Breach the IT administrator's VPN account -------> Access the heart of a big corporate network.
4. Trick a government worker into sharing secrets -------> Use the information to simplify the next attacks.

# Attacks on Humans

Attacks targeting humans share a common trait: 

they **rely on manipulating the victim into helping the attacker**, whether knowingly or not. This tactic is known as **social engineering**, and it works by 
exploiting human psychology rather than technical flaws.

Tactics:-
1. Trustworthy- victim finds attackers legitimate.
2. Emotional- triggers fear, curiosity or urgency.

## Phishing Attacks

Email phishing is the most common form of social engineering.
You receive an email ---------> When you click on the link provided i it---------> it takes you to a **fake login page** that  
looks real, ------------------> but **sends your credentials to the attacker**.

## Malware Downloads
Downloading files to install an application------> the file may be a malware.
To make these attacks more successful, threat actors use innovative techniques like fake CAPTCHAs, malicious QR codes, and SEO poisoning (manipulate search engine 
rankings to push malicious websites to the top of search results).

## Deepfakes

The rapid rise of **AI-generated video or audio has become more effective in impersonating** family members, colleagues, or corporate partners

## Impersonation

Aattackers are quite successful in pretending to be someone else (impersonating someone). Many recent ransomware attacks started with a simple phone call from 
attackers impersonating a corporate IT department. 

## Other Attacks

1. Insider threat
2. Physical Attack
3. USB drop Campaigns(leaving behind malicious flash drives, USB  in public or corporate areas(restroom,office cafeteria, parking lots etc.) to exploit human curiosity and compromise targeted computer systems.)

Defending against threats involves two key tasks: **Mitigation and Detection**.

Mitigation aims to prevent or reduce the chance and impact of attacks.

A SOC analyst's task is to detect and investigate attacks. The never ending task of detecting and investigating is tiresome. This is why knowing key mitigation measures is essential. If mitigation ideas put forward by SOC are approved by IT and top management, it will ease the SOC routine and make the company more secure.
